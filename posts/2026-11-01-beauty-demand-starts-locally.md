<p>A tidy global demand number can conceal a messy local reality. A shade may sell through quickly in one country while its companion shade sits in a retailer&#8217;s stockroom elsewhere. A travel size may work through pharmacy distribution while a larger pack depends on department store traffic. Blend these signals too early and the aggregate looks calm while shoppers face gaps in some channels and excess in others. Beauty planning should protect the evidence behind each demand signal.</p>
<p>L&#8217;Oréal describes universalisation as globalising brands while taking regional characteristics into account, with respect for differences between consumers. That principle is useful for planning: a shared global view can coordinate decisions, but it must leave room for why a product moves differently by country, channel, shade, pack size, season, or promotional context. Local variation is information to reconcile, not noise to remove.</p>

<h2>Keep local demand visible</h2>
<p>Start by separating sell in from sell through. A shipment into a distributor can indicate commercial activity, but it does not necessarily show consumers buying the product. Temporary inventory build can make sell in look strong while downstream demand remains ordinary. Sell through is closer to consumer movement, though it still needs context about store coverage, online availability, and replenishment timing.</p>
<p>Promotion creates another trap. A discount, feature, sampling campaign, or retailer placement can produce a sharp lift that should not automatically become the new baseline. The planner should record the event, duration, affected channels, and products that benefited, then distinguish temporary promotion lift from a persistent change in preference. A local team may know that a spike reflects a campaign ending soon, while a global aggregate sees only an attractive growth rate.</p>
<p>Availability can shape demand signals: stockouts suppress observed sales, while substitutions may mean the purchased item differs from the customer’s original preference. To interpret history responsibly, document stock availability, outages, replenishment constraints, and assortment changes alongside sales. This context helps distinguish genuine demand shifts from sales patterns created by what was offered.</p>
<p>Geography reinforces the need for detail. L&#8217;Oréal reported like for like sales growth of 4.4% in Europe and 0.5% in North Asia for 2025, at comparable scope and exchange rates. These figures do not show that any forecasting tool caused the difference. They show why a global average is too blunt for decisions about local demand, channel exposure, and confidence in a market signal.</p>

<h2>Make local evidence comparable</h2>
<p>Local evidence becomes useful globally when teams describe it in a consistent structure. A practical record can identify the SKU, country, channel, period, observed measure, known event, availability condition, and confidence level. This common language does not flatten the market. It lets a country team explain a deviation in terms another team can understand and challenge without silently replacing the original context.</p>
<p>Shared assumptions matter as much. Teams should agree how to treat promotions, launches, substitutions, returns, missing data, and unusual channel closures. Local overrides should remain possible, but each should state its reason and an expiry date. An override linked to a temporary promotion should age out differently from one supported by repeated sell through across several channels. Otherwise exceptions become permanent and the plan loses its connection to evidence.</p>
<p>Planner attention should go first to items where the signal can change a decision: popular shades with constrained availability, packs whose sales may be displaced by substitutions, products concentrated in one channel, and launches or seasonal items with little history. Stable replenishment items can receive a lighter review. Spend scarce time where availability, channel concentration, or uncertain preference could alter the shared plan.</p>
<p>Consider a hypothetical beauty planner preparing a regional view. The global file shows a healthy increase for a complexion range, so the initial instinct is to spread the uplift across nearby countries. Review shows that one market&#8217;s movement is concentrated in two shades and one online channel, while a second market has weak availability for those shades. The planner keeps those facts separate, records promotion and availability effects, and recalculates the shared view without exporting the first market&#8217;s pattern everywhere.</p>
<h2>Turn differences into accountable decisions</h2>
<p>Reconciliation works best when ownership is explicit. Country teams should own the quality and explanation of local signals. Category or regional teams should test whether those explanations fit the wider pattern. A global demand owner should resolve conflicts in assumptions and make the final shared view understandable. Disagreement then has a route to resolution instead of disappearing inside an averaged number.</p>

<p>Review timing should match the signal. A sudden deviation may need a short cycle when availability or promotion is changing, while a stable category can be reviewed less frequently. Each review should ask what changed, whether the change is visible in sell through, which channels are affected, and whether the evidence should alter the shared plan or remain a local exception. This creates responsiveness without turning every movement into a universal uplift.</p>
<p>A focused pilot makes the approach testable. Choose one beauty category and two adjacent markets with different channel mixes. Preserve SKU, country, and channel detail, document promotions and availability, apply common assumptions, and allow dated local overrides. After the first review cycle, compare decisions made with and without local evidence, examine unresolved disagreements, and check whether availability improved without avoidable excess. The planner should then adjust the rules, ownership, or review cadence before expanding the pilot.</p>
