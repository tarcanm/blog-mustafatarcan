<p>Goods can leave a factory before the holiday and still arrive too late to become usable stock. Start with the date inventory must be usable at destination, then work backward through receiving, customs, inland transport, vessel cutoff, booking and factory completion. Crosscheck the earliest supplier promise against actual need and stock cover. A delay to a completed shipment belongs to the route and destination timeline; a factory restart matters to the next replenishment, when materials, components or quality release may still be incomplete.</p>
<p>Lunar New Year falls on February 6, 2027, eleven calendar days earlier than February 17, 2026. That earlier date compresses the period before factories and logistics networks move into holiday preparations, but it does not create one closure date for every supplier. Ask each supplier for its closure, last workable ship date, material status and expected restart point.</p>

<h2>Start with usable inventory</h2>
<p>The planning question is when the product can be counted as usable inventory, not merely when a factory can finish a purchase order. The destination may need customs clearance, inland movement, inspection, quality approval and warehouse receiving before the stock is available for sale or production. Each handoff belongs in the backward calculation. Check the route assumptions against the destination calendar, warehouse capacity and release process before treating the date as firm. The earliest reliable replenishment date is useful only when it is compared with the SKU&#8217;s need date and current stock cover.</p>
<p>Seko Logistics said relatively few factories had announced their 2027 calendars. For the northern factories Seko works with, the company described planned closures from January 25 to February 15. That regional example should prompt supplier specific questions, rather than being applied to all of China.</p>
<p>Separate two kinds of risk. Before the holiday, the concern is whether production can finish and whether the completed goods can depart on the planned route. After production, departure still depends on booking, terminal cutoffs, vessel schedules, customs and inland transport. A later replenishment may then face a different risk: the factory has reopened, but materials, components, subcontractors or quality release are not yet ready. A factory restart cannot delay a shipment that already left; it can affect the next replenishment.</p>

<h2>Confirm the last workable departure</h2>
<p>Once the destination availability date is fixed, calculate the last workable completion and departure for each supplier. Include the port or terminal cutoff, planned vessel, customs, inland delivery, receiving and checking time. February 6 being earlier than February 17 is a calendar signal, not a transferable lead time. The operational effect depends on the supplier, product, route and available stock cover.</p>
<p>Seko Logistics advises shippers sourcing from China to bring purchase orders forward and book ocean freight at least four weeks before shipment. Early booking creates room to compare sailings and respond to changes before the preclosure rush. It does not reserve production, guarantee vessel space or prove that a supplier will meet its completion date. Keep the purchase order, factory plan and freight booking as separate gates.</p>
<p>Confirm the dates in terms a planner can use: the last workable ship date, materials required before closure, the expected restart point and the earliest reliable replenishment date. Treat reopening as the beginning of verification, rather than instant normal output. Seko also recommends considering one to two additional weeks of transit contingency. Use that as planning room, not as a predicted delay or a performance guarantee.</p>
<h2>Buy cover without buying trouble</h2>
<p>In an explicitly illustrative case, a planner gives a critical replacement component and a slow-moving accessory the same order timing. Supplier checks then show that the critical component has a less reliable restart than the slow-moving accessory. The planner can separate the component&#8217;s required cover from the accessory&#8217;s demand and storage position. That makes the decision more useful than moving both purchase orders forward by the same amount.</p>

<p>The component may justify an earlier order and a confirmed booking after its material and closure dates are checked. The accessory may remain on its normal path until demand and destination cover support earlier delivery. This exposes the tradeoff between supply protection, uncertain demand, working capital, storage space and obsolescence risk at SKU level.</p>
<p>Review the decision with finance and commercial colleagues where demand is uncertain. Record the usable inventory date, supplier dependency, stock cover and point at which the plan must be revisited. An early order should address a clear availability risk, rather than simply reflect an earlier holiday calendar.</p>
<p>This week, reconfirm each supplier&#8217;s last workable ship date and earliest reliable replenishment date. Reconcile critical SKU cover with finance and commercial teams, then check that any proposed early order has a feasible booking and destination receiving plan. The immediate test is whether the stock can be usable when needed, with enough room for the risks that remain after production.</p>
